Theta correspondence

The theta correspondence is a way to transfer irreducible representations between two different groups which form a dual pair inside a symplectic group. This correspondence has a global version as well as a local one; here we focus on the latter: we work with smooth (complex) representations of ppp-adic groups. Suppose GGG and HHH are two such groups forming a dual pair. Given an irreducible representation π\piπ of GGG, we would like to compute its theta lift θ(π)\theta(\pi)θ(π), which is a representation of HHH.

Dual pairs come in two flavors: type I (these involve the classical groups) and type II (general linear groups). The local theta correspondence for type II dual pairs has been explicitly described by Minguez [5]. For type I dual pairs, the work of Atobe and Gan [1] describes θ(π)\theta(\pi)θ(π) when π\piπ is tempered. In [2], this was extended to a description of θ(π)\theta(\pi)θ(π) for arbitrary π\piπ.

The purpose of these programs is to demonstrate the results of [2].

Background/Notation​

Groups​

To simplify notation, we focus on a specific dual pair: Sp(W)×O(V)\text{Sp}(W) \times \text{O}(V)Sp(W)×O(V).

The correspondence between other dual pairs of type I is entirely similar, and is covered in [2].

We work over a ppp-adic field FFF. Because dual pairs have symmetric roles in the correspondence, we try to keep the notation symmetric. To that end, let ϵ∈{±1}\epsilon \in \{\pm 1\}ϵ∈{±1}. Let

Wn= a (−ϵ)-symmetric vector space of even dimension nVm= an ϵ-symmetric vector space of even dimension m\begin{split} W_n &= \text{ a } (-\epsilon)\text{-symmetric vector space of even dimension }n \\ V_m &= \text{ an } \epsilon\text{-symmetric vector space of even dimension }m \end{split}Wn​Vm​​= a (−ϵ)-symmetric vector space of even dimension n= an ϵ-symmetric vector space of even dimension m​

Let G=G(Wn)G=G(W_n)G=G(Wn​) (resp. H=H(Vm)H=H(V_m)H=H(Vm​)) denote the group of isometries of WnW_nWn​ (resp. VmV_mVm​).

This is just a fancy way of saying the following: if ϵ=1\epsilon = 1ϵ=1, then GGG is symplectic and HHH is orthogonal. The roles of GGG and HHH are switched when ϵ=−1\epsilon = -1ϵ=−1.

Splitting​

To set up the theta correspondence, one needs to fix a splitting of the metaplectic cover over G×HG \times HG×H. This involves characters χV\chi_VχV​ and χW\chi_WχW​ of F×F^\timesF×, see [1, §\S§2.8]. We only mention this because these characters show up throughout the results; however, they do not affect the combinatorics that govern the theta lifts.

Parameters of tempered representations​

The results of [1] are stated in terms of the Local Langlands Classification. Recall that the Langlands parameter of a representation π\piπ of GGG can be viewed as a representation ϕ\phiϕ of WF×SL2W_F \times \text{SL}_2WF​×SL2​, where WFW_FWF​ denotes the Weil group of the field FFF. We can always decompose this representation into irreducibles, which look like ρ⊗Sk\rho \otimes S_kρ⊗Sk​; here ρ\rhoρ is an irreducible representation of WFW_FWF​, and SkS_kSk​ is the unique irreducible kkk-dimensional representation of SL2\text{SL}_2SL2​.

Remark

The theta correspondence only cares about ρ⊗Sk\rho \otimes S_kρ⊗Sk​ if ρ=χV\rho = \chi_Vρ=χV​ and kkk is odd. Therefore, you will not need to enter the entire representation π\piπ -- just the relevant part.

The parameter ϕ\phiϕ corresponds to an LLL-packet. To identify the representation itself, more information is needed. We need a character of a certain component group attached to the parameter (see [1, §\S§3]), which amounts to putting a sign on each of the irreducible pieces of ϕ\phiϕ.

Standard modules​

Recall that essentially square-integrable representation of the general linear group correspond to segments [ρνa,ρνb][\rho\nu^a, \rho\nu^b][ρνa,ρνb], where ρ\rhoρ is a unitary cuspidal representation and a,ba, ba,b are real numbers such that b−ab-ab−a is a non-negative integer.

Let τ\tauτ is a tempered representation of a classical group. A standard module is any representation of the form δk×⋯×δ1⋊τ,\delta_k \times \dotsb \times\delta_1 \rtimes \tau,δk​×⋯×δ1​⋊τ, where

  • each δi\delta_iδi​ is an essentially square-integrable GL\text{GL}GL-representation, attached to [ρiνai,ρiνbi][\rho_i\nu^{a_i}, \rho_i\nu^{b_i}][ρi​νai​,ρi​νbi​];
  • ak+bk≥⋯≥a1+b1>0a_k+b_k \geq \dotsb \geq a_1 + b_1 > 0ak​+bk​≥⋯≥a1​+b1​>0.

Here we use the standard notation for parabolic induction (see [2, §\S§2.4]); furthermore, we allow k=0k=0k=0.

Any irreducible representation of GGG is the unique irreducible quotient of a uniquely determined standard module. The results of [2] are stated in terms of the standard module of π\piπ.

Remark

Again, you will not need to enter the entire standard module of π\piπ -- just the segments with ρ=χV\rho = \chi_Vρ=χV​.

Relative level​

We introduce a bit of notation that's slightly tricky, but highly useful: if π\piπ is a representation of G(Wn)G(W_n)G(Wn​), the lift of π\piπ to H(Vm)H(V_m)H(Vm​) will be denoted by θℓ(π)\theta_{\ell}(\pi)θℓ​(π), where ℓ=n+ϵ−m\ell = n + \epsilon - mℓ=n+ϵ−m.

Note that lll is always odd, because mmm and nnn are both even.

If you object to this notation, here are a few reasons why you should become a fan:

  • This is the notation used in [2].
  • This notation allows us to forget nnn and mmm. We already said that the theta correspondence only sees a certain part of the parameter of π\piπ. Consequently, it is not nnn or mmm that are relevant; it is their difference!
  • By definition, θℓ(θ−ℓ(π))=π\theta_{\ell}(\theta_{-\ell}(\pi)) = \piθℓ​(θ−ℓ​(π))=π, whenever θ−ℓ(π)≠0\theta_{-\ell}(\pi)\neq 0θ−ℓ​(π)=0.

Towers​

If π\piπ is a representation of the symplectic group, there are two target towers of orthogonal groups we can lift to. If π\piπ is a representation of an orthogonal group, we can still speak of two towers; see [1, §\S§4.2] or [2, §\S§2.10].

On one of the towers, the lifts start occurring early; on the other, late. (This is a consequence of the Conservation Relation, and can be made precise). Following [1], we refer to the early (late) occurrence tower as the going-down (going-up) tower.

Remark

Identifying the going-up/going-down tower is a subtle problem; we do not discuss it here. The going-up/going-down tower for π\piπ is inherited from the tempered part of its standard module τ\tauτ; the problem for tempered representation is resolved by [1, Theorem 4.1 (2)].

Theta elevator user manual​

To use the Theta elevator, you need to

  • Enter π\piπ
  • Choose the tower (up/down) you want to lift to; see Notation→Towers.
  • Choose the relative level ℓ\ellℓ you want to lift to; Notation→Relative Level

To enter π\piπ, you will need to enter information about its standard module: you will enter the segments which correspond to δi\delta_iδi​'s, as well as the Langlands parameter of τ\tauτ.

Entering τ\tauτ​

The parameter of τ\tauτ breaks up into irreducibles of the form ρ⊗Sk\rho \otimes S_kρ⊗Sk​; recall that we are only interested in the terms with ρ=χV\rho = \chi_Vρ=χV​. Therefore, you only need to enter the kkk's that appear. Moreover, recall that each of these terms comes with a sign ±\pm±. To enter the corresponding information, we use a hack: instead of entering kkk and its sign separately, we allow kkk to be negative. (Caveat: all isomorphic terms necessarily have the same sign.)

Enter the information about the parameter of τ\tauτ by entering a comma-separated list of (possibly negative!) integers. Repetition is allowed.

Example 1

Entering [-1,3,-5] implies that the parameter of τ\tauτ contains χVS1−⊕χVS3+⊕χVS5−.\overset{-}{\chi_VS_1} \oplus \overset{+}{\chi_VS_3} \oplus \overset{-}{\chi_VS_5}.χV​S1​−​⊕χV​S3​+​⊕χV​S5​−​. Note that the parameter might contain other irreducibles, but these are the only ones that are relevant.

Example 2

(repetition). Entering [-1,3,-5,3] implies that the parameter of τ\tauτ contains χVS1−⊕χVS3+⊕χVS3+⊕χVS5−.\overset{-}{\chi_VS_1} \oplus \overset{+}{\chi_VS_3} \oplus \overset{+}{\chi_VS_3} \oplus \overset{-}{\chi_VS_5}.χV​S1​−​⊕χV​S3​+​⊕χV​S3​+​⊕χV​S5​−​.

Example 3

(non-example). Entering [-1,3,-5,-3] will result in an error: there are two different signs associated with S3S_3S3​.

Entering segments​

Again we only need information about the segments [ρνa,ρνb][\rho\nu^{a}, \rho\nu^{b}][ρνa,ρνb] for which ρ=χV\rho = \chi_Vρ=χV​. Enter these segments by entering a (semicolon-separated) list of ordered pairs:

(a1,b1);(a2,b2);… ;(ak,bk)(a_1,b_1); (a_2,b_2); \dotsc; (a_k,b_k)(a1​,b1​);(a2​,b2​);…;(ak​,bk​)

The list doesn't have to be sorted in any specific way.

An example​

We use an example to explain the input/output of the Theta Elevator.

  • Tempered parameter for π\piπ: -1,3,-5
  • Standard module segments for π\piπ written in quotient notation: (1,3); (3,4); (5,5)
  • Tower: Going up tower
  • ℓ\ellℓ: 15

The notation we are using allows us to suppress the information that does not affect the recipe for the lift; we are only entering the relevant parts of τ\tauτ and the standard module. In praticular, note that we don't even need to know which group we are starting from (symplectic or orthogonal).

For example, the parameter we entered for τ\tauτ is

χVS1−⊕χVS3+⊕χVS5−\overset{-}{\chi_VS_1} \oplus \overset{+}{\chi_VS_3} \oplus \overset{-}{\chi_VS_5} χV​S1​−​⊕χV​S3​+​⊕χV​S5​−​

which looks like a parameter for Sp(8)\mathrm{Sp}(8)Sp(8). However, this could just be a part of a larger parameter, possibly of an orthogonal group.

Similarly, the segments we entered to describe the standard module are only the relevant ones -- there could be other segments, but those have ρ≠χV\rho \neq \chi_Vρ=χV​. (Keep in mind that the segments we entered are those with ρ=χV\rho = \chi_Vρ=χV​.)

Remark

There is a small amount of information that we lose by choosing to disregard the non-important part of the parameter, and this is related to the signs on the irreducible parts of σ\sigmaσ. If one is lifting in the symplectic → orthogonal direction, the signs are correct. If the lift is orthogonal → symplectic, the signs might need to be flipped (this depends on the target tower). Similarly, if ℓ(τ)=−1\ell(\tau) = -1ℓ(τ)=−1, then the parameter of σ\sigmaσ is obtained by simply adding S1S_1S1​ to the parameter of τ\tauτ. The sign attached to this S1S_1S1​ is more-or-less arbitrary in this program, because we are missing the information needed to determine it.

For the purposes of this demonstration, the effect of these minor inaccuracies is outweighed by the benefits of simplifying the parameters. For a fully precise statement of the results on lifts of tempered representations, one should consult [1].

Remarks​

  • The quick and dirty introduction to the theta correspondene presented here should not be taken too seriously. Kudla's notes [4] offer a great introduction (and much more). For a historical overview see [3]. A good reference for the Local Langlands Correspondence is the Appendix of [1].
  • The question of which tower is going up/down is ignored in the current version of this program. I intend to address this in the next version.
  • Also left for a future version are the various dual pairs of type I that are not addressed here. I hope this current version will succeed in making the results of [2] more accessible.
